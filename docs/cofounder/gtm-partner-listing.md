## What I've built

A subscription platform that turns ephemeral digital art into a physical, collectible ritual.

- **Creators** publish curated _releases_ (from their existing back-catalog + new work) and earn recurring, transparent payouts.
- **Collectors** subscribe to artists they already follow and receive premium printed booklet "drops" on a recurring cadence — instead of art that disappears into a feed.
- **I handle the hard part for the artist:** printing, shipping, VAT, and customer service are fully outsourced to a print-on-demand partner. The creator never touches inventory.

The whole loop works today: public creator pages, collector onboarding, subscriptions, release selection, transparent pricing, billing, and automated print fulfillment. **This isn't an idea looking for an engineer — it's a working product looking for distribution.**

## Why now / why it matters

Digital and AI artists are stuck. Their income is scattered across Patreon, Ko-fi, Civitai, tips, and commissions; platforms take opaque cuts and change the rules overnight; AI artists face stigma and disclosure drama; and their work lives and dies by an algorithm. Meanwhile their fans have no way to _keep_ the art they love.

We give artists one durable, transparent, recurring revenue stream tied to their own audience — and give fans something worth owning. The wedge is **creator-led**: each artist brings their own audience via a shareable page, so growth compounds artist-by-artist rather than through expensive paid traffic.

The behavior we depend on is already proven: **Patreon alone pays out ~$24M/month to ~280k creators from 10M+ paying patrons** — fans clearly pay creators on a recurring basis. We're turning that recurring support into something physical and collectible.

Capturing a _fraction of a percent_ of this is a real business (see below).

### How the money splits (read this before the scenarios)

A collector's payment splits **three ways**, and you should understand the order:

1. **Print + shipping + VAT** (the print partner + tax) — the bulk of the price.
2. The leftover is the **margin pool**.
3. That pool is split **creator / platform**. The 50/50 partnership applies to _our slice only_ — the platform fee, not the whole transaction.

On a 30-page booklet at today's deliberately low pricing:

| Collector pays | Print + ship + VAT | Margin pool | → Creator (90%) | → **Platform (10%)** |
| -------------- | ------------------ | ----------- | --------------- | -------------------- |
| €18.54         | €16.20             | €2.34       | €2.11           | **€0.23**            |

So at launch settings the platform keeps ~€0.23/booklet, and the partner's half of that is ~€0.12. **That is intentionally near-zero** — it's priced creator-first (90/10) to win supply, and two of its inputs are dials we turn up as the brand earns the right to:

- **Platform split** — planned to step from 90/10 at launch toward 80/20 at scale (≈2× our slice).
- **Price** — a 30-page booklet retails €18.54 today vs. **€25–150 for comparable softcover art/photo books**. Almost the entire gap is pure margin. Pushing retail from €18.54 → ~€28 grows the margin pool from €2.34 to ~€11.80 (**~5×**) — and that's still cheaper than a typical art book.

Per active subscriber / month, those three states look like:

| Pricing state                | Collector pays | Margin pool | Platform slice | **Partner's ½** |
| ---------------------------- | -------------- | ----------- | -------------- | --------------- |
| Launch (90/10, €18.54)       | €18.54         | €2.34       | €0.23          | €0.12           |
| Scaled split (80/20, €18.54) | €18.54         | €2.34       | €0.47          | €0.23           |
| Repriced (80/20, €28)        | €28.00         | €11.80      | €2.36          | €1.18           |

### Bottom-up: what the engine generates at volume

One booklet per subscriber per month. "Current pricing" uses the scaled 80/20 split at €18.54; "with pricing power" uses €28 retail — the lever the GTM/positioning partner owns.

| Scenario       | Creators × avg subs | Subscribers | Annual GMV | Platform rev/yr (current) | Platform rev/yr (repriced) | **Partner's ½ (repriced)** |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ----------- | ---------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Early traction | 50 × 80             | 4,000       | ~€0.9M     | ~€22k                     | ~€113k                     | **~€57k**                  |
| Working engine | 300 × 120           | 36,000      | ~€8M       | ~€200k                    | ~€1.0M                     | **~€510k**                 |
| At scale       | 1,500 × 150         | 225,000     | ~€50M      | ~€1.3M                    | ~€6.4M                     | **~€3.2M**                 |

The "at scale" row is **~0.2% of DeviantArt's users, or ~1% of Midjourney's** — a sliver of one community. A further multiplier on every row: more than one drop per cycle.

**The honest read:** per-booklet margins are thin _by design_, so this is a **volume + pricing** game, not a high-margin-per-unit one. At today's creator-first pricing the platform's slice is tiny; the value you'd create is (1) volume and (2) earning the pricing power to widen the margin pool toward what the artifact is actually worth. The engine is built and the demand behavior is proven — what's missing, and what you'd own, is exactly those two levers.

## My role (what I cover)

- Product development and ongoing maintenance — the platform is mine to build and run
- Print-on-demand fulfillment management — production specs, provider relationship, quality, logistics
- Everything technical and operational behind the scenes

**What I'm not good at — and don't want to fake — is distribution.** That's the other half of the company.

## Who I'm looking for

You'd own everything that gets creators and collectors _onto_ the platform:

- **Creator acquisition** — finding and recruiting digital/AI/illustration/concept/tattoo-flash artists with engaged audiences (1k–100k followers), and helping them launch their first release
- **Positioning & messaging** — sharpening how we talk to artists vs. collectors across Reddit, Instagram, DeviantArt, Discord, Civitai
- **Growth experiments** — outreach motions, referral/share loops, conversion, retention; figuring out which creators and channels actually produce paying collectors
- **The collector side** — turning artist audiences into subscribers

**You're a strong fit if you:**

- Genuinely care about digital art — AI _and_ traditional — and get why a physical, curated artifact matters
- Have done GTM / growth / community / creator-marketing before (or have run your own audience and know the artist world from the inside)
- Are comfortable with hands-on, unglamorous early-stage outreach, not just strategy decks
- Want to build the distribution engine, not be handed one

**Probably not a fit if** you're looking for a salaried marketing role, need a pre-existing growth machine to optimize, or aren't excited to do founder-level scrappy outreach yourself.

## The offer

- **50/50 split of platform revenue.** You own GTM, I own product + fulfillment — equal partners on the platform's slice (the "Partner's ½" columns above). To be exact: revenue splits print/VAT → creator → platform first; we split the platform slice 50/50.
- **Honest about the stage:** this is pre-revenue. The split is a revenue-share partnership, not a salary and not (yet) formal equity — we can formalize structure as traction and the relationship prove out. I'd rather be straight about that than oversell it.
- **What's already de-risked:** the product exists and works, and the demand behavior is proven — so your effort goes straight into traction instead of waiting on a roadmap or validating the core bet.
