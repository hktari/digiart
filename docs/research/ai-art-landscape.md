## Verified core (high confidence — cleared 2-0 adversarial votes)

1. **"Explorable worlds" is an institutionally-validated curatorial frame.** MAK Vienna (major applied-arts museum) ran a 4-month exhibition — _"/imagine: A Journey into The New Virtual"_ (May–Sept 2023) — organized around a **four-part taxonomy**:
   - Speculative Narratives & Worldbuilding
   - Research Investigations
   - Dreamscapes
   - AI & Algorithmic Variation

   This maps almost directly onto magazine chapters. **Bonus: it happened in Vienna** — our proposed beachhead — so it doubles as a local credibility anchor in the pitch.

2. **Speculative architecture & imagined environments are a real, professionally-practiced category** — not hobbyist noise. Landscape architects (Hassell / Jon Hazelwood) use Midjourney to generate "nature-positive," biodiverse urban-future imagery as purposeful worldbuilding. Aspirational, beautiful, awe-oriented → **prime venue-safe content.**

_(Source: mak.at, thenewvirtual.org, architectureau.com)_

## Indicative findings (single-source / unverified — treat as leads)

- **Print precedent exists.** _The AI Art Magazine_ — biannual, ~176–180pp, ~€22, independently funded, launched Dec 2024 (Mike Brauner); editions through Apr 2026. Validates a **coffee-table-scale format at a mid-tier price**, framed as "print as a chronicle of a fast-moving moment."
- **Market precedent exists.** Christie's _"Augmented Intelligence"_ (2025), first major all-AI auction (34 works).
- **Hotels already use AI art as ambient decor**, and the field's own guidance favors calming categories: **landscapes, seascapes, geometric/abstract patterns, imagined-nature**.
- **Creator exemplars (venue-safe end):** Lynn Kim / _LynnSquared_ — photoreal "step-right-into-it" dreamscapes (imaginary resorts, space, architecture); _GetAgent_ — landmarks reimagined in alternate styles.

## The central risk: reputational backlash

Commercializing/exhibiting AI art **repeatedly provokes serious backlash** (all indicative but consistent across 4 outlets):

- Christie's auction → **6,000+ artist petition** (uncompensated training data / "theft").
- Gallery show _"No Permission Needed"_ (2025) → ~200 negative comments ("slop, unethical"), **3 artists withdrew** in protest.
- University AI exhibit → **physically attacked**, ~⅓ of works destroyed.
- Artsy 2026 survey → only **9%** of gallery pros see AI art as a legitimate medium; **25%** call it "destabilizing."

**This is the #1 thing the product design must defuse.**

## Venue-fit flags → editorial rules

### ✅ GREEN — lead with these (beautiful, awe-oriented, artifact-resistant)

- Imagined **landscapes & natural environments** (rewilded, terraformed, alien-but-serene)
- **Speculative-but-livable architecture** & imagined cities (aspirational, not dystopian)
- **Cosmic / celestial vistas**
- **Abstract / generative pattern & color**
- **Seascapes**, imagined flora

_Why: these read as calming ambient decor, and AI artifacts (bad hands, uncanny faces) barely surface in non-figurative/environment imagery._

### 🟡 AMBER — use sparingly, art-directed

- **Creatures / bestiary** (great "worlds" hook, but keep whimsical, not grotesque)
- **Retrofuturism / mythology** (charming, but watch for cliché "AI-slop" coding)
- **Characters** at middle-distance / stylized (never uncanny close-up realism)

### 🔴 RED — avoid for ambient hospitality

- **Close-up photoreal human portraits/figures** — uncanny valley + anatomy defects (six-finger hands)
- **Dystopian / decayed / ruin** scenes (e.g. Marcus Byrne's decayed architecture) — unsettling as decor
- **Disembodied eyes / fractured human forms** surrealism — off-putting in public space
- **Anything overtly provocative or "AI!"-shouting** — invites the theft/slop discourse

## Concrete implications for the magazine

1. **Adopt a MAK-style chapter spine:** e.g. _Worldbuilding · Dreamscapes · Imagined Architecture · Cosmic · Algorithmic Abstractions_. It's coherent, gallery-grade, and every chapter sits in the GREEN/AMBER zone.
2. **Lead with the aesthetic experience, not "made by AI."** The wonder is the hook; the tech is a footnote. This is also why "Worlds" (curiosity) beats a tech-forward pitch for venues.
3. **Inoculate against backlash with provenance — and this is our unfair advantage.** The entire controversy is about _uncompensated, non-consensual_ AI art. **Our platform is the opposite: named artists opt in, are credited, and are paid** (existing creator-payout rails). A visible "made by paid, credited artists — not scraped" line on the masthead flips the biggest risk into a differentiator. **This may be the single most important positioning decision.**
4. **Screen every spread** against the RED list before print — a curation checklist, not just vibes.
5. **Format target:** coffee-table-scale, premium print (matches _AI Art Magazine_ precedent and your existing "quiet luxury / gallery-catalog" aesthetic).
