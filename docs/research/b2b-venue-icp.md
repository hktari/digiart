# B2B Venue ICP — "Worlds" AI-Art Magazine

**Status:** draft / pre-validation
**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Product:** a curated print magazine of *explorable AI-generated worlds* — a browsable coffee-table object placed on venue tables/counters/lobbies for guests to leaf through. Sold to the venue on a recurring basis. See [b2b-venue-channel](./b2b-venue-channel.md).

---

## The job the magazine does *for the venue*

It makes the space feel **curated, current, and conversation-worthy** with zero effort, gives guests something tactile to do (dwell), and signals the venue is culturally switched-on. Secondary: it's Instagrammable and (later) can earn the venue commission on print sales.

## The five qualifying dimensions

A great prospect scores high on all five. This is the filter behind the segment ranking.

1. **Dwell time** — guests physically sit/wait long enough to pick something up and browse (≥15 min).
2. **Aesthetic curation** — the venue already spends deliberate effort on how it looks/feels (design-forward, not utilitarian).
3. **Clientele receptivity to AI art** — the crowd finds imagined worlds / concept art *cool*, not threatening. **This is the make-or-break qualifier for this product** and it's what separates it from the ambient "Feeling" product.
4. **Budget + reachable decision-maker** — there's an ambiance/experience spend line and an owner/manager who can say yes without a committee (favors independents over chains).
5. **Discoverability at scale** — the venue is findable and filterable via public signals (Maps category, Instagram, review volume) so the lead-scraper can source them.

## Segment ranking (tightest fit first)

| Rank | Segment | Dwell | Aesthetic | AI-receptive clientele | Why they say yes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Co-working / creative & startup spaces** | High | High | **Very high** (tech/creative crowd) | Member perk; "we're an innovative, creative space"; the AI angle is a *feature* here, not a risk |
| **2** | **Specialty / third-wave coffee shops** | High | High | High | Talking point + dwell + "we're curated/cool"; owners already host local art |
| **3** | **Boutique / design hotels** | High (lobby, room, lounge) | Very high | Medium–high | Guest experience, on-brand differentiation, higher budget, coffee-table object |
| **4** | **Tattoo studios & upscale barbers/salons** | Very high (captive waiting) | Medium–high | High (art-loving crowd) | Keeps waiting clients engaged; art-aligned brand; concept-art overlap is strong |
| **5** | **Concept/lifestyle retail, design stores, indie bookshops** | Medium | Very high | High | Doubles as a **sellable product** (reseller angle) + browse object |
| **6** | **Board-game / hobby / "geek" cafés, comic shops** | High | Medium | Very high (sci-fi/fantasy natives) | Perfectly on-theme with imagined worlds/lore |
| 7 | Wine bars / cocktail lounges | Medium | High | Medium | Design-conscious but dark/social → low reading |
| 8 | Boutique Airbnb / short-term rentals | High | High | Medium | Coffee-table object, but curation buyer is fragmented (hosts/PMs) |

**Deliberately excluded:** fast-casual chains, fine-art galleries (crowd often ideologically anti-AI-art), fast-food, medical waiting rooms with mass-market clientele — low on aesthetic, receptivity, or decision-maker access.

## The ideal prospect — green flags

Within a good segment, prioritize venues that show:

- **Independent or micro-chain** (1–3 locations) → owner/manager decides fast
- **Strong, deliberate Instagram** with aesthetic/interior content → they care about atmosphere and can amplify
- **High review volume + solid rating (4.4★+)** → real foot traffic and dwell
- **Existing curation cues** — local art on walls, a bookshelf, records, plants, design objects, a "concept" identity
- **Younger / creative / tech-adjacent clientele** (signalled by neighborhood, price tier $$–$$$, aesthetic)
- **Located in a creative-dense urban pocket** (design district, university/startup area)

## Red flags — deprioritize or skip

- Corporate chain / franchise (procurement, no local budget)
- Utilitarian or grab-and-go format (no dwell, no ambiance spend)
- Clientele likely hostile to AI art (traditional fine-art, craft-purist scenes)
- No web/social presence (unreachable and probably not aesthetic-led)
- Dark/loud/social-only rooms where nobody reads

## Who actually signs

- **Independent café/studio/shop:** the **owner** (often also the aesthetic director). One conversation.
- **Boutique hotel:** **GM or F&B / guest-experience manager**.
- **Co-working:** **community/experience manager** or founder.
Pitch to the person who owns *how the place feels*, not procurement.

## Geography — beachhead

Start **local and dense**: Vienna / Austria first (matches existing Peecho EU shipping economics and lets you hand-deliver sample issues), then expand to one or two creative EU cities (Berlin, Amsterdam, Lisbon). Walk-in-able radius matters for the validation phase.

## Detectable signals → lead-scraper sourcing recipe

To let `lead-scraper` source these at scale, filter public data on:

- **Google Maps category** = coffee shop / coworking space / boutique hotel / tattoo studio / bookshop
- **Rating ≥ 4.4** and **review count ≥ 150** (traffic + dwell proxy)
- **Price tier** = $$–$$$
- **Has Instagram** linked, with **interior/aesthetic** imagery (not just product shots)
- **Keyword hits** in name/description/reviews: "specialty", "third-wave", "design", "concept", "curated", "creative studio", "independent"
- **Independent** (not a known chain brand string)
- **Geo-fenced** to beachhead city districts

Output per lead: name, segment, decision-maker guess, Instagram handle, aesthetic-fit note, walk-in address.
